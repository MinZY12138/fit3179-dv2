01 / THE NATIONAL PICTURE

A growing workforce.
A changing balance.

Has teaching capacity kept pace with student enrolments? Over 2006–2025, teacher FTE grew faster than student FTE. Comparing growth from the same starting point makes that difference visible.

Full-time equivalent measures working capacity. Two teachers each working half-time contribute one FTE. It should not be read as a headcount.

02 / WHERE TEACHERS WORK

One country.
Different teaching contexts.

A national average combines eight different jurisdictions. Ratios help compare teaching capacity relative to student enrolments, while sector comparisons reveal differences within each state and territory.

03 / THE PEOPLE BEHIND THE PROFESSION

The workforce has
more than one profile.

ABS describes school staffing capacity. The Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership (AITSL) provides a survey perspective on the people in the profession. The populations and reference years differ, so these measures are presented separately.

AITSL survey estimates describe participating teacher groups; they are not an exhaustive census. They must not be combined with ABS FTE as though the populations were identical.

04 / WORKFORCE PRESSURES

Counting teachers
doesn't count the workload.

A growing national workforce can coexist with demanding working conditions. Self-reported hours and reasons for considering departure describe pressures that staffing totals alone cannot capture.

Workforce growth and individual experience answer different questions. Neither the survey measures nor the national totals alone identify local vacancies or predict departures.

THE THREAD THAT CONNECTS THE NUMBERS

More capacity is progress.
It is not the whole picture.

Teacher FTE grew faster than student FTE nationally from 2006 to 2025. That broad pattern sits alongside differences between jurisdictions and school sectors. AITSL's survey perspective adds another dimension: teaching capacity depends on people whose roles, career stages and working conditions vary.

These sources support a careful reading of workforce change. They do not establish why every difference exists, identify local vacancies, or predict who will leave. Understanding those questions requires more detailed evidence.

Sources & methods

ABS Schools, 2025: Tables 51a (teaching staff FTE), 43a (student FTE) and 53a (published ratios). Other in-school staff are excluded. Student data are subject to ABS confidentiality adjustment. Methodological changes can affect comparisons over time.

AITSL National Trends: Teacher Workforce, June 2026: exact published tables and numerical key findings, primarily reporting 2024. Survey percentages use weighted counts to adjust demographic and jurisdictional representation. In 2024, 48,068 teachers responded to at least part of the survey (8.5% of registered teachers). These estimates and rounded percentages are not an exhaustive census. Some workforce groups include early childhood settings; charts specify their scope.

Australian state boundaries and representative points: ABS ASGS Edition 3 (2021), CC BY 4.0. The geographic maps use a conic equal-area projection centred on Australia, with standard parallels at 18°S and 36°S. The cartogram rescales projected outlines by the square root of FTE divided by outline area, then separates them without changing relative areas. Its positions are schematic.
